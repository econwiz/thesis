Koichiro Ito, 2014

The standard model of nonlinear budget sets predicts that consumers optimize x based on the true marginal price schedule p(x). 

First empirical strategy is to examine bunching of consumers at the kink points of nonlinear price schedules. According to the marginal price analysis, consumers should bunch around kink points, trying to stay on one side before a large step in rates. However, Ito finds no bunching even when shifting to a 5-tier rate schedule.

Consider that consumers have consumption xit and face a nonlinear price schedule p(xit). Define a series of surrounding consumption levels by xk,it = (1+k/100)xit, Let pk,it = p(xk,it) denote the marginal price for xk,it. Consumers may care about surrounding marginal costs because of uncertainty or inattention. Consumers construct their percieved price by deciding on relative weights wk on pk,it. Suppose there is price variation-- using this, he estimates wk by observing how consumers respond to changes in pk,it. Then, he can use the estimates of wk to recover the shape of consumers' perceived prices. 

Among time-invariant electricity pricing, therefore, the most efficient pricing is likely to be the flat marginal rate that equals the marginal cost of electricity.
